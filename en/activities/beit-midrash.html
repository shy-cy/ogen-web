About the class

The Bible, Big Questions and Fresh Perspectives

We’re delighted to welcome Yifat Hald as the leader of OGEN Centre’s Community Beit Midrash.

Yifat is a familiar and much-appreciated member of the community, known for her engaging approach to Bible study, combining curiosity, depth and a fresh perspective on familiar stories.

The Community Beit Midrash brings adults and teenagers together in one group for a shared learning experience. Parents are welcome to join with their teenage children, but adults and teenagers are equally welcome to attend on their own.

Together, we’ll revisit familiar biblical stories and discover that they are far more complex and fascinating than they might first appear. We’ll explore different versions of these stories, compare sources, and ask questions about identity, faith, society and gender — while considering what these ancient texts can tell us about the world we live in today.

This is an open, thought-provoking and community-centred learning experience, where different generations can exchange ideas, ask questions, listen to one another and respectfully disagree.

No previous knowledge of the Bible is needed. Just bring your curiosity and a willingness to see familiar stories in a new light.

Six sessions. Ancient stories. Questions that still matter today.

During the six sessions, we’ll explore:

Two Stories, One Creator

The two biblical accounts of creation and what their differences reveal.

A Flood of Stories

The two flood accounts and the fascinating connections between Genesis and the Epic of Gilgamesh.

Jewish DNA

The legacy of our biblical ancestors and what their stories tell us about Jewish identity.

The Soul of a King

The light and shadows in the life of King David, one of the Bible’s most complex and fascinating figures.

The Paradox of Ecclesiastes

From deep despair to practical optimism — and what the Book of Ecclesiastes can teach us about life.

Created in God’s Image or from His Rib?

The story of the creation of woman, its different interpretations, and what it might tell us about gender roles at home.
